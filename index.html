Methodology and Limitations

What This Metric Measures

This metric estimates annualized temple endowment sessions per member of record. It counts sessions, not unique attendees; one person can attend multiple times. Living and proxy sessions are combined for the geographic attendance rates.

Geographic rate = (trailing 7-day endowment sessions × 365 ÷ 7) ÷ average estimated membership over those same 7 days.

Daily Membership Estimates

Each U.S. state and country with at least two reported membership years has a separate daily membership estimate. Membership is linearly interpolated between the Church's published year-end counts. After the latest published year, the most recent annual absolute change is used to project membership forward. Seven-day rates use the average of those seven daily estimates; the current geography maps use the average over the corresponding reporting window. A new annual snapshot replaces projections with interpolated historical estimates. Estimates are not independently observed daily headcounts.

Living Endowment Eligibility Proxy

The historical living-endowment chart uses a different denominator: an estimate of the annual cohort becoming eligible for a first living endowment. For eligibility year Y, it adds the Church's new children of record from year Y − 18 to convert baptisms from year Y − 1. These annual statistical-report counts are lagged to approximate when those cohorts first become eligible under an 18-year age threshold and a one-year convert waiting period.

Living rate = (trailing 7-day living endowments × 365 ÷ 7) ÷ average estimated annual eligibility cohort over those 7 days × 100%.

The annual eligibility-cohort values are known through 2026 using 2008 children of record and 2025 converts. Daily values are interpolated between known year-end cohort totals, then projected beyond the latest known total. The annual updater incorporates newly released statistical-report figures, which supply the next eligibility year. Unlike membership projections, adding the 2026 report in 2027 does not change the already-known 2026 eligibility anchor.

Important: This is a proxy for new annual eligibility, not an exact count of people who receive or qualify for a living endowment. Children of record are not all necessarily baptized, convert baptisms can include minors, and individuals may delay or never receive an endowment. The resulting percentage is not the observed proportion of eligible people receiving an endowment.

Rolling Window and Annualization

Trailing seven-day windows smooth day-to-day variation. Annualizing the recent pace does not imply that activity will continue at that pace for a full year. The current day is excluded because its activity data may be incomplete.

Comparing Multiple Geographies

For a continent, the denominator includes all countries and territories with published membership figures in that continent, whether or not they have a temple. Countries without recorded temple activity contribute zero sessions but remain in the denominator. This also applies to the selected-group trend and comparison table; unchecking a country or state removes its membership and activity. Each U.S. state is counted once, so the United States is not counted a second time as a country. The worldwide reference uses the Church's separate worldwide membership total, including members living where country-level figures are not published; the U.S. reference uses all 50 states. These reference lines remain independent of the selection filters. Countries with only one annual membership figure temporarily contribute their reported count to aggregate denominators, but no standalone historical per-member trend is estimated until a second figure becomes available.

Sources and Limitations

Temple activity counts are sourced from Fuller Consideration's endowment history. Membership figures and living-eligibility inputs are based on the Church's published annual statistical reports and country/state statistics. Annual source snapshots are preserved; automatic updates are validated before affecting rates. Geography is assigned by the temple's physical location.

Geographic comparisons are approximate and do not directly measure individual participation or religious commitment. Attendance may include visitors from outside a geography; for example, Illinois can receive visits from out-of-state members attending the Nauvoo temple.
